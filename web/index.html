Claude, ChatGPT or any MCP client gets its own Solana wallet and an allowance. It pays APIs over x402, trades any token through Jupiter, and writes every action to a public registry anchored on chain. Over budget, it stops. Revoked, it stops.

How a trade works

Asked in words, checked in code, proved on chain.

  1. Ask

    "Put $2 into WIF." The agent resolves the ticker on Jupiter and refuses tokens that can still be frozen.

  2. Quote

    Jupiter prices it on mainnet liquidity. The agent checks its budget and the slippage cap.

  3. Swap

    It re-prices, aborts if the market moved past the floor, then signs with its own key.

  4. Record

    A signed line in the registry, rolled into a Merkle root posted on Solana.

Tools your agent gets

  
Seven tools. That's the kit.

  

    
ToolWhat it does

    
allow_walletIts address, balance, holdings and trading mode.

    
allow_budgetCaps, what's used, what's left. It checks before spending.

    
allow_payFetches a URL and pays its x402 price in USDC (no SOL needed, the seller pays the fee) or SOL.

    
allow_quotePrices a buy or sell of any token on Jupiter. Nothing moves.

    
allow_swapExecutes the quote, under the floor it showed.

    
allow_token$ALLOW on chain: supply, price, market cap, largest holders.

Questions

  
Before you hand it the keys.

  
Can the agent raise its own budget?
No. None of the seven tools writes the allowance. The owner controls answer only on this machine, never through a tunnel.

  
Does the agent need SOL?
Not to pay. x402 payments in USDC are signed by the agent and submitted by the seller, who pays the network fee. A few lamports of SOL are only needed to anchor the registry on chain and to trade.

  
Who holds the key?
You do. It's a file in ~/.allow on your machine. Allow runs no custody server and never sees it.

  
Does it really trade?
Yes, through Jupiter on mainnet when you switch live mode on. Until then every swap fills at the live Jupiter quote into a paper wallet and is marked simulated in the registry.

  
What stops a honeypot?
Tokens whose creator can still freeze holders are refused. Thin liquidity, open mint and concentrated holders come back as warnings in the quote.

  
How is the registry public?
Each entry is hash-chained and signed by the agent. Batches are rolled into a Merkle root posted as an SPL Memo signed by the same wallet. /verify checks all of it against a Solana RPC from your browser.
